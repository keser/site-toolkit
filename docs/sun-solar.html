Computed with the NOAA solar-position algorithm (declination + equation-of-time series, ±0.01° accuracy) for the site's exact coordinates, using tools/sun_arcs.py, the same series as the map set's sun path sheets. The two equinoxes share a panel: September is the solid line, March the dashed one. Their arcs nearly coincide, but March runs about 15 minutes later on the clock. Sunrise and sunset are almanac times (the sun's centre 0.833° below the horizon, for refraction and the sun's radius). Times are local clock time (EST in December, EDT in March/June/September). House roof — solar resource

Lidar: CT State GIS Office, 2023 statewide flight (bare earth and maximum surface, 2 ft cells), via CT ECO. Sun positions: tools/sun_arcs.py, same series as the charts above; shading by ray-stepping through the surface with tools/shade.py. The flight was leaf-off, but lidar still records branches, so deciduous trees block here as if in leaf: spring and fall sun at the garden is a lower bound. Roof TSRF (total solar resource fraction) is clear-sky direct sun on each roof cell, weighted by its tilt and facing, as a share of an unshaded panel at 35° facing south. It is a relative measure for siting panels; an output estimate in kWh needs a weather-based model such as PVWatts. The roof's outer 2 ft is left out.

Findings for design

What this part of the profile means for a design, sorted as strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats. The top findings roll up into the site-wide synthesis.

Strengths

  • Summer sun reaches 71.9° at noon with over 15 hours of daylight; east- and west-facing spots get low-angle sun morning and evening.

Weaknesses

  • Winter sun peaks at 25.0°, so anything to the south casts a long shadow.
  • Trees, roofs, and fences block direct sun at the vegetable garden for part of the day (see Garden Solar Analysis).

Opportunities

  • Limbing the pines and oaks raises the canopy line and lets low winter sun reach the ground.
  • The roof solar-resource map shows where panels would work.

Threats

  • Neighboring houses and trees on both sides shade the lot and are outside the owner's control.
