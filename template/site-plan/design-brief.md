# Design brief

Who the design is for, what they want, and the design layers that answer it. The as-is analysis
is in `site-profile.md`; tasks live in `tasks.md`.

Treat each layer as a working draft. Expect to revise it as the site profile fills in and the
stakeholders react.

---

## People

**Answers**
- Who decides scope, budget, and timeline? Who else is affected but doesn't decide?
- Who does the work: owners, volunteers, contractors? Which tasks need a professional or a permit?
- What does each person care about, practically and emotionally?

**Collect**
- An interview with each decision-maker: how they use the site now, what frustrates them, what
  they picture in five years, what they won't give up. Record it with permission and keep the
  transcript private.
- Plants, structures, or places with family or cultural meaning.

**Watch for**
- Meaningful plants can be invasive. Default to keeping the plant and removing the spread: take
  cuttings or pot a contained specimen, then remove the spreading population. Explain the ecology
  before asking for a decision. Buy-in for one plant doesn't carry over to the next.
- Neighbors are notified about boundary work. They are not decision-makers. Refer to them by role.

## Design layers

Each layer starts as a brainstorm on the grayscale base map, then becomes proposed map sheets
(`NNb`, next to the current-state `NNa`).

### Zones and placement
- Using the goals table, which zone should each wanted element sit in, based on how often it's
  visited and what it feeds or needs?
- Which elements belong on the edge between two zones?
- How does the layout respond to the sectors, microclimates, and the strengths and weaknesses found
  in the profile?

### Resilience
- Which elements would help the site ride out the hazards in profile section 5?

### Water
- What are the biggest water challenges and opportunities?
- Where do tanks, rain gardens, swales, or ponds go, which zone is each in, and where does each one
  overflow?
- Is everything sized for the design storm?

### Soil
- What does each test site need: compost, cover crops, chop-and-drop, mulch, decompaction,
  inoculants?
- Where do compost and mulch come from, on site and off?

### Thematic areas and succession
- What distinct areas does the site divide into (orchard, pollinator garden, gathering space, …)?
- For each area: hold its successional stage, speed it up, or let it slow down?

### Plant systems
For one thematic area at a time:
- A detailed planting plan that shows why each plant goes where it goes.
- A species table: scientific and common names, ecological role, layer, size, light and water
  needs.
- A perspective view, so the stakeholders can see it.
- How the system works as a whole, and the maintenance it needs through the year.

Placement certainty comes before speed for trees and slow perennials. Fill cheaply: buy one or two
plants, let them establish, and propagate to fill the area, unless erosion or habitat needs it
covered now.

### Animals
- Where do domestic animals fit, and what do they cycle (fertility, pest control)?
- Which habitat features help wildlife: water, cover, nesting, corridors?
