Site-wide findings

The strongest findings from across the profile. Each one links to the page it comes from.

Strengths

  • Outside the 100-year floodplain, with low fire and crime risk. Flood
  • Rain in every month, on a lot that is 87% permeable; at the back, turf, mature trees, and a needle-mulched spruce grove soak up most of what reaches them. Water
  • A wide range of microclimates on 0.43 acres. Zones
  • Native species already thriving, next to a regional wildlife corridor. Species

Weaknesses

  • Invasive vines and shrubs have overgrown Zone 5 and the property lines. Zones
  • Plantings are circumstantial; the most-visited zones produce little. Zones
  • The lawn drives runoff, and the street-side corner collects water from the driveway, roof, and sidewalk before it reaches the storm drains. Water
  • Close neighbors and their trees limit sun and privacy. Sectors

Opportunities

  • One buffer planting can handle winter wind, summer ozone, noise, and privacy together. Sectors
  • Clearing produces mulch, wood chips, and firewood on site. Zones
  • Soil building, swales, and roof barrels can keep rain on the lot. Water
  • Limbing recovers low winter sun under the canopy. Sun

Threats

  • Ice storms and wind (6/10) on weak limbs over paths and structures. Sectors
  • Design storms of 8+ in, with water arriving from upslope to the west. Water
  • Deer browse on new plantings. Sectors
  • Invasives keep spreading without active control. Species

Open questions before design

  • Is there a real view line worth keeping open? Check on site, with leaves on and off. Sectors
  • What does Portland's animal ordinance allow for coop setbacks, flock size, and roosters? Sectors
  • How does runoff split between the street and the backyard? It hasn't been measured. Water
  • Tree and building heights in the 3D sun model are estimates until measured. Sun

Design ideas to test

Ideas the owner raised while studying the site, carried forward from the zone brainstorm. Each still has to hold up against the findings above.

  • Move the wood pile and work area beside the driveway, so deliveries no longer cross a neighbor's driveway.
  • Shade the pool deck and back path with fruiting vines instead of a built pergola.
  • Add seating in the Zone 2 shade garden, so it becomes a place to stay rather than pass through.
  • Replace the southeast boxwood hedge with a pollarded willow row for fencing material and mulch.
  • Rebuild a three-bin compost system in Zone 4, and pollard the maples for a steady supply of chips.
  • Move to raised beds in Zone 3, and move the shed to the shaded area by the coop.
